WHY THE PRODUCT EXISTS

The difficult part isn't drawing a room. It's remembering what the room means.

Architectural concepts survive a long sequence of client requests, geometry edits, design constraints and handoffs. Archis explores a project state that can keep revisions, relationships and approval decisions together rather than treating every change as a brand-new plan.

01 / STATE

One building, multiple views.

Spaces, geometry, levels, constraints and project context belong to a canonical state. The 2D and 3D interfaces consume that state instead of maintaining unrelated copies.

02 / REVIEW

Approval before mutation.

Proposals are represented as reviewable operations. The architect can edit, accept or reject them; stale proposals cannot silently overwrite later edits.

03 / ACCOUNTABILITY

History with consequences.

Accepted changes can be replayed and reverted, including branch-local behavior. A dependency-aware revert can refuse an unsafe reversal.

What is actually implemented?

The working Archis repository describes a semantic project model, linked 2D/3D views, editing and CAD tools, deterministic constraints, blueprint interpretation with review, versioning, local persistence and exports including SVG, DXF and GLTF. This case study deliberately narrows its interactive claim to revision and review behaviors covered by inspected tests.

What is still experimental or unvalidated?

Automatic inference of architectural intent and semantic-distance ranking remain heuristic. Blueprint reconstruction is partial and requires correction; it is not lossless CAD conversion. Real architect workflow impact, reliable professional BIM interoperability, structural/MEP/code compliance and production-ready end-to-end AI extraction require separate validation.

How should this work be evaluated?

Start with actual architect-authored revision histories. Measure time to acceptable revision, manual corrections, rejected proposals, preservation of architect-confirmed relationships and whether users return for a second real project. A polished demo is not evidence of product-market fit.
